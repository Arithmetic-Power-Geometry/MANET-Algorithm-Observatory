# Academic Section Naming

## Principle

Headings in the eventual paper should describe the scientific question or analytical function. Internal project language such as "Bible", "best", "ultimate", "revolutionary", "observatory magic", or "no one has written" must never appear in manuscript headings or claims.

## Preferred main-section vocabulary

Use:
- **Evolution and Design Rationale**
- **Unified Taxonomy**
- **Evidence Base**
- **Comparability**
- **Reproducibility**
- **Experimental Methodology**
- **Applicability**
- **Robustness**
- **Failure Frontiers**
- **Research Gaps**
- **Research Agenda**
- **Threats to Validity**

Avoid:
- Ultimate comparison
- Best algorithms
- Winning protocols
- Complete truth
- Revolutionary taxonomy
- Unprecedented survey

## Preferred subsection pattern for algorithm families

1. Design Motivation
2. Decision Mechanism
3. Information and Resource Requirements
4. Empirical Evidence
5. Limitations and Failure Conditions
6. Research Obligations

## Table-title pattern

Prefer noun phrases that identify the analytical object:
- "Comparability of Quantitative MANET Routing Studies"
- "Evidence Maturity Across Routing Families"
- "Implementation Provenance of Benchmark Candidates"
- "Propagation and Mobility Assumptions in Comparative Evaluations"

Avoid vague titles such as "Comparison Table" or "Summary of Papers."

## Figure-title pattern

Figure titles/captions should state what is mapped:
- "Evolution of MANET Routing as Design Responses to Prior Limitations"
- "Coverage of the MANET Routing Evaluation Space"
- "Condition-Dependent Changes in Protocol Ranking"
- "Evidence-Backed Failure Frontiers"

## Abstract vocabulary

The abstract should use:
**survey**, **systematic evidence synthesis**, **taxonomy**, **comparability**, **reproducibility**, **controlled benchmark**, **applicability**, **research obligations**.

Use **novel** only for a clearly specified contribution whose closest-prior-work comparison supports the term.
