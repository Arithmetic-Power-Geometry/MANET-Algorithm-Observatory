# COMST Claim Language

## Evidence strength verbs

Use **establishes/demonstrates** only when direct evidence is sufficiently strong for the exact claim.

Use **indicates/suggests/is consistent with** when evidence is limited, conditional, heterogeneous, or indirect.

Use **reports** for claims attributed to an external study.

Use **we observe under the evaluated conditions** for Observatory experimental results.

## Comparative claims

Do not write:
"Protocol A is better than Protocol B."

Write:
"Under the specified mobility, traffic, propagation, and scale conditions, the evaluated implementation of Protocol A exhibited [metric effect] relative to Protocol B."

## Literature gaps

Do not equate sparse literature with a failed capability.

Use:
- underexplored evaluation region;
- reproducibility limitation;
- unresolved contradiction;
- robustness evidence gap;
- demonstrated capability gap.

## Novelty

Novelty statements require closest-prior-work evidence. Until that audit is frozen, use:
"The Observatory is designed to provide..."
rather than:
"This is the first..."

## Generalization

Every empirical conclusion must identify whether it is:
- mechanism-level;
- implementation-level;
- scenario-level;
- benchmark-level;
- literature-level.

Cross-level generalization requires explicit justification.
