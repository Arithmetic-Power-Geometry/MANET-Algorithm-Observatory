# COMST-Level Paper Architecture

## Design constraint

The main paper must behave like a navigation instrument. The repository carries exhaustive status data; the paper carries the highest-information synthesis.

## Proposed narrative

### I. Why MANET routing needs an evidence atlas
Explain fragmentation: algorithms are commonly compared under incompatible mobility, propagation, scale, traffic, information, and cost assumptions.

### II. How to read the MANET routing design space
Teach seven axes:
1. acquisition timing;
2. information horizon;
3. routing decision;
4. mechanism;
5. resource cost;
6. evidence maturity;
7. unresolved obligation.

### III. From classical routing to intelligent routing
Causal evolution, not chronological catalogue:
problem → mechanism → capability → cost → next branch.

### IV. Unified mechanism taxonomy
Map classical, geographic, multipath, QoS, energy, security, optimization, ML, RL, MARL, GNN, predictive, federated, and cross-layer approaches into the same ontology.

### V. What the literature actually establishes
Evidence maturity and evaluation coverage.

### VI. Why published algorithm rankings disagree
Comparability, mobility, propagation, metric definition, implementation, tuning, seed/statistical, and information-budget effects.

### VII. Reproducibility and deployability
Code, configurations, seeds, statistics, testbeds, field evidence.

### VIII. The Observatory benchmark
Admission, fairness, benchmark contract, controlled experiments.

### IX. Applicability and failure frontiers
Condition-dependent results, Pareto views, ranking stability, failure boundaries.

### X. Research obligations
Evidence-backed unresolved questions and, if justified, the capability gap motivating a later novel algorithm.

### XI. How to design and evaluate the next MANET routing algorithm
A concise reusable checklist derived from the evidence.

### XII. Conclusion
What is known, conditionally known, weakly supported, contradictory, and unresolved.

## Main-paper visual budget

Target approximately 10–12 high-information figures and 10–14 compressed tables, subject to readability/page constraints. Combine related panels only when they answer one question.

## Supplement/repository split

Main paper:
- conceptual maps;
- compressed evidence summaries;
- key benchmark/frontier results;
- highest-value status tables.

Repository/supplement:
- full corpus;
- all algorithm cards;
- complete evidence matrix;
- search records;
- extended benchmark results;
- machine-readable data;
- generated artifact manifests.
